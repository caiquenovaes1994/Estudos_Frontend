Inner Solar System

Mercury

Messenger of the Gods

Distance from Sun 57.9M km (0.39 AU)
Equatorial Diameter 4,879 km
Orbital Period 88 Earth Days
Rotation Day Length 58.6 Earth Days
Surface Temp -180°C to +430°C
Known Moons 0

Planetary Profile

Mercury is the smallest planet in our solar system—only slightly larger than Earth's Moon. Because it has virtually no atmosphere to trap heat, nighttime temperatures plunge dramatically.

Key Explorations

NASA's Mariner 10 provided the first close-up images, followed by the MESSENGER orbiter which mapped its scarred surface. ESA and JAXA's BepiColombo mission is currently en route.

Inner Solar System

Venus

The Morning & Evening Star

Distance from Sun 108.2M km (0.72 AU)
Equatorial Diameter 12,104 km
Orbital Period 225 Earth Days
Rotation Day Length 243 Earth Days (Retrograde)
Surface Temp 465°C (Average)
Known Moons 0

Planetary Profile

Often dubbed Earth's "twin" due to similar mass and radius, Venus took a radically different evolutionary path. Crushing surface pressures equal deep ocean depths on Earth, hot enough to melt lead.

Retrograde Rotation

Venus rotates in the opposite direction to most planets. On Venus, the Sun rises in the west and sets in the east, and its day is longer than its entire year.

Earth

The Blue Marble

Distance from Sun 149.6M km (1.00 AU)
Equatorial Diameter 12,742 km
Orbital Period 365.25 Days
Rotation Day Length 23h 56m 04s
Surface Temp -88°C to +58°C
Known Moons 1 (The Moon)

Planetary Profile

Earth is the fifth largest planet in our solar system and the only world known to host living organisms. It boasts a dynamic geology driven by plate tectonics, abundant liquid water, and a shield of magnetosphere.

Our Lunar Companion

The Moon stabilizes Earth's axial wobble, moderating climate extremes over millions of years and creating ocean tides that shaped the evolution of life.

Inner Solar System

Mars

The Red Planet

Distance from Sun 227.9M km (1.52 AU)
Equatorial Diameter 6,779 km
Orbital Period 687 Earth Days
Rotation Day Length 24h 37m (1 Sol)
Surface Temp -140°C to +20°C
Known Moons 2 (Phobos, Deimos)

Planetary Profile

Mars is a terrestrial planet with a thin atmosphere of carbon dioxide. Its iron-rich surface rusts into reddish dust. Vast canyons like Valles Marineris dwarf Earth's Grand Canyon.

Robotic Exploration

Rovers such as Curiosity and Perseverance, together with orbital probes, have uncovered compelling evidence of ancient river deltas, organic molecules, and subsurface water ice.

Outer Solar System

Jupiter

Giant of the Worlds

Distance from Sun 778.5M km (5.20 AU)
Equatorial Diameter 139,820 km
Orbital Period 11.86 Earth Years
Rotation Day Length 9h 55m
Atmosphere Temp -110°C (Cloud top)
Known Moons 95 (Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto...)

Planetary Profile

Jupiter contains more than twice the mass of all other solar system planets combined. Its rapid rotation squashes the planet into an oblate spheroid and creates jet streams of swirling ammonia clouds.

Galilean Moons

Volcanic Io erupts sulfur fountains, while Europa conceals a vast subsurface salty ocean containing more water than all of Earth's oceans combined.

Outer Solar System

Saturn

The Jewel of the Solar System

Distance from Sun 1.43B km (9.54 AU)
Equatorial Diameter 116,460 km
Orbital Period 29.45 Earth Years
Rotation Day Length 10h 33m
Atmosphere Temp -140°C
Known Moons 146 (Titan, Enceladus, Dione...)

Planetary Profile

Saturn is the second-largest planet, yet so low in density that it could theoretically float on water. Its majestic rings extend up to 282,000 kilometers from the planet, yet are remarkably thin—often less than 10 meters thick.

Remarkable Moons

Titan possesses a dense nitrogen atmosphere with lakes of liquid methane and ethane. Enceladus sprays water geysers into space from an underground ocean through south-polar fissures.

Outer Solar System

Uranus

The Sideways Ice Giant

Distance from Sun 2.87B km (19.2 AU)
Equatorial Diameter 50,724 km
Orbital Period 84 Earth Years
Rotation Day Length 17h 14m (Retrograde)
Atmosphere Temp -224°C (Minimum)
Known Moons 28 (Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel...)

Planetary Profile

Discovered in 1781 by William Herschel, Uranus was the first planet found with a telescope. Its striking blue-green color stems from atmospheric methane, which absorbs red light.

Extreme Tilt

With an axial tilt of 97.77°, its poles experience 42 years of continuous sunlight followed by 42 years of darkness as it journeys around the Sun.

Outer Solar System

Neptune

The Windy Blue Giant

Distance from Sun 4.50B km (30.1 AU)
Equatorial Diameter 49,244 km
Orbital Period 164.8 Earth Years
Rotation Day Length 16h 06m
Atmosphere Temp -218°C
Known Moons 16 (Triton, Proteus, Nereid...)

Planetary Profile

Neptune is more than 30 times as far from the Sun as Earth. It was mathematically predicted before being observed in 1846 by Johann Galle based on gravitational perturbations in Uranus' orbit.

Triton's Cryovolcanism

Its giant moon Triton orbits backwards in a retrograde path, indicating it was likely a captured Kuiper Belt object with nitrogen geysers that erupt into space.

Kuiper Belt Realm

Pluto

Guardian of the Kuiper Belt

Distance from Sun 5.91B km (39.5 AU)
Equatorial Diameter 2,376 km
Orbital Period 248 Earth Years
Rotation Day Length 153.3 Hours (6.4 Days)
Surface Temp -230°C to -223°C
Known Moons 5 (Charon, Nix, Hydra, Kerberos, Styx)

Planetary Profile

Discovered by Clyde Tombaugh in 1930, Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006. In 2015, NASA's New Horizons spacecraft revealed a dynamic, geologically active world of nitrogen ice plains and rugged water-ice peaks.

Binary System with Charon

Charon is half Pluto's size, meaning both bodies orbit a shared center of mass (barycenter) floating above Pluto's surface, mutually locked face-to-face.
